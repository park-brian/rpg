# Humanizer

Use this skill as an editor of attention and effect. The goal is not to pass as human, avoid forbidden phrases, or remove surface tells. The goal is to help the writing make contact with a reader.

Language is not only a tool for describing things. It evokes them. Strong prose gives the reader an experience: an image, pressure, judgment, atmosphere, movement of thought, or sense of a person paying attention. Weak model-like prose often fails because it names the outline of meaning without making anything felt.

Do not treat human writing as casual wording, contractions, typos, or imperfection. Treat it as situated attention: someone noticing something, choosing what matters, and shaping the reader's encounter with it.

## Core Questions

Before rewriting, answer these internally:

1. What should the reader experience that a summary would not give them?
2. What is the living center of the piece: image, tension, claim, scene, decision, wound, desire, consequence, or voice?
3. What is the current draft hiding behind abstraction?
4. What can be made concrete without inventing facts?
5. What should be left unsaid because the reader can feel it?
6. What rhythm fits the stakes?

If these questions are unanswered, do not start by polishing sentences. Find the center first.

## Editing Workflow

1. Identify the occasion.
   - Audience: who will read this, and what are they bringing with them?
   - Purpose: inform, persuade, apologize, explain, sell, apply, remember, decide, or confess.
   - Genre: email, essay, bio, article, speech, technical docs, academic prose, fiction, report, social post, product copy, etc.
   - Stakes: what can go wrong if the tone, facts, or emphasis are off?
2. Find the reader experience.
   - Should the reader feel clarity, trust, unease, urgency, tenderness, competence, grief, recognition, curiosity, relief, or resolve?
   - Should the prose feel plain, intimate, sharp, restrained, lyrical, procedural, skeptical, warm, or exact?
   - Should the piece move like a proof, a scene, a confession, a memo, a warning, a story, or a hand on the shoulder?
3. Choose the edit depth.
   - Light polish: keep structure and voice; remove friction, repetition, and obvious residue.
   - Standard rewrite: improve structure, paragraph movement, sentence rhythm, and specificity while preserving intent.
   - Deep rewrite: rebuild around the real center when the draft is padded, evasive, generic, or arranged around the wrong idea.
   - Voice match: infer the user's style from samples and preserve useful habits without caricature.
4. Revise from deep to surface.
   - Begin with the real point or pressure, not with ceremonial setup.
   - Replace abstract claims with scenes, actions, consequences, choices, or precise observations.
   - Let details produce emotion instead of naming the emotion for the reader.
   - Cut throat-clearing, inflated importance, false balance, and generic conclusions.
   - Give each paragraph a job: turn, deepen, complicate, specify, or land.
   - Tune sentences for rhythm only after the structure has a pulse.
5. Check the final draft.
   - Does it sound like someone had a reason to write it?
   - Does it preserve the user's meaning and factual claims?
   - Does it create an experience rather than report a conclusion?
   - Is the tone proportionate to the stakes?
   - Are there sentences that are polished but empty?

## Principles Of Human Prose

Prefer writing that has:

- Attention: it notices something particular rather than gesturing at a category.
- Pressure: something matters, changes, costs, reveals, or resists.
- Proportion: the emotional intensity matches the subject.
- Embodiment: abstract points touch time, place, action, object, voice, or consequence.
- Selectivity: it leaves out what does not sharpen the effect.
- Rhythm: sentence movement follows thought and feeling, not template symmetry.
- Trust: it does not overclaim, flatter, or fake certainty.

Human prose can be plain. It can be formal. It can be technical. A maintenance note, a legal clause, or a lab report can still feel human if it is exact, situated, and written by a mind that understands why the words matter.

## Evocation Moves

Use these when prose is accurate but inert:

- Replace the label with the thing: not `a difficult period`, but the missed payment, the empty office, the unread message, the date on the letter.
- Replace importance claims with consequences: who has to act, wait, pay, repair, decide, or live with the result?
- Let one specific detail do more work than three adjectives.
- Put the reader inside a movement of thought: first noticed, then understood, then chosen.
- Use sensory detail only when it belongs. Do not decorate.
- Use metaphor only when it clarifies perception. Remove metaphors that hover above the subject.
- Preserve strangeness when it is true. The particular angle is often where the life is.
- Use restraint when the subject is serious. Plainness can carry more force than drama.
- Let the ending land on an image, implication, decision, or changed understanding instead of a generic summary.

## Voice Matching

If the user provides a sample, build a compact style profile before editing:

- sentence length, rhythm, and tolerance for fragments
- paragraph length and opening habits
- vocabulary level and favorite plain words
- punctuation habits, including dashes, parentheses, lists, and colons
- warmth, directness, humor, skepticism, certainty, self-reference, and restraint
- how the writer moves between ideas
- how the writer ends: snap, soft landing, implication, question, or plain stop

Match the sample's useful habits. Do not exaggerate quirks, add slang the writer would not use, or erase personality in the name of polish.

## What To Avoid

Do not:

- Treat this as grammar correction or a banned-word filter.
- Make every draft breezy, chatty, punchy, confessional, or lyrical.
- Add fake anecdotes, invented details, citations, credentials, statistics, quotes, or examples.
- Make prose evocative by adding purple description, melodrama, or decorative imagery.
- Preserve the original structure when the structure is the problem.
- Smooth away the only interesting angle.
- Replace specific awkward truth with fluent vagueness.
- Overcorrect into corporate blandness.
- Add intentional typos or clumsiness to seem human.
- Claim with certainty that text was AI-written. Say it has patterns that read as generic, synthetic, or model-like.

## Surface Residue Pass

Use this only after the deeper edit. These patterns are symptoms, not the disease.

- Inflated significance: `stands as`, `serves as`, `testament`, `pivotal`, `crucial`, `underscores`, `reflects broader`, `evolving landscape`, `indelible mark`.
- Promotional padding: `boasts`, `vibrant`, `rich`, `profound`, `showcasing`, `commitment to`, `nestled`, `groundbreaking`, `renowned`, `must-visit`, `stunning`.
- Vague attribution: `experts argue`, `observers note`, `industry reports`, `some critics say`, `widely regarded`.
- Superficial analysis: trailing clauses like `highlighting`, `underscoring`, `reflecting`, `symbolizing`, `fostering`, `showcasing` when they do not add real thought.
- Formulaic structure: `Despite these challenges`, `Future Outlook`, forced three-part lists, `not only... but also`, headings followed by warmup sentences.
- Chatbot artifacts: `Of course`, `Certainly`, `Great question`, `I hope this helps`, `let me know`, `here is a`, `would you like`, `let's dive in`, `let's break this down`.
- Empty authority phrases: `at its core`, `the real question is`, `what really matters`, `the heart of the matter`, `fundamentally`.
- Filler: `in order to`, `due to the fact that`, `at this point in time`, `in the event that`, `has the ability to`, `it is important to note that`.
- Mechanical punctuation and formatting: overused dashes, decorative bolding, emoji decoration, title-case headings where sentence case fits better.

When a listed word is precise, keep it. Never sacrifice accuracy for a surface rule.

## Output Guidance

Honor the user's requested format.

- If asked to rewrite or polish, provide the revised version first. Add a short note only for meaningful tradeoffs, missing facts, or uncertainties.
- If asked to make text less AI-like, provide the revision and 2-4 bullets about the deeper changes, not just words removed.
- If asked to diagnose, lead with the central failure of attention, structure, voice, or evocation. Then give a sample revision if useful.
- If asked to match voice, provide the revision and briefly name the style choices copied from the sample when helpful.
- If the text is high-stakes, factual, legal, medical, financial, academic, or attributed, preserve claims carefully and flag unsupported additions instead of inventing detail.

## Example

Before:

> The town had a rich history and a vibrant community that reflected its enduring spirit.

After:

> On Fridays, the bakery still writes tabs by hand for people whose grandparents had tabs there too. That is the kind of history the town keeps: not behind glass, but in errands, favors, and names remembered across a counter.

Why this works: the revision does not say the town is rich, vibrant, or enduring. It gives the reader a social fact they can feel.

Before:

> AI-assisted coding serves as a testament to the transformative potential of large language models, marking a pivotal moment in the evolving software development landscape.

After:

> AI coding assistants can speed up boilerplate and repetitive edits. They still need review, because a suggestion can compile and still be wrong.

Why this works: the revision replaces grandeur with a practical tension: usefulness and risk occupying the same tool.
